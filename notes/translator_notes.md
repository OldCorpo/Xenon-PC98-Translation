Translator notes on Xenon
=========================

There's two worlds on the game.

Allegedly the real world, where everyone is Japanese and every girl have a proper Japanese Kanji Name, you can agree that is Japan at a contemporary time. In this world Kouji is written as 宏治.

And the dream world, where nobody seems to be Japanese and every Name is on Katakana, you have Tracy that is the busty American girl, Bianca that I assume is Italian (by the name), Farneria who who's know she's from (can be Venus or any other planet, it doesn't matter, she's a psychic, def not an Earthling), and Lanhua that is Chinese 100%. In this world Kohji is written as コウジ.

Farneria is an alien girl who can see the colors of the emotions of every living thing, to make it more understandable, I simplified the concept to "She can see aura colors".

There's a lot of borrowed lingo in the game, examples:
- "Calvador" is a type of wine as far I know, found on "The Quadra is a Calvador-class light mobile ship"
- "Quadra" goes on the style of "Quattro Bajeena" a main character of Z Gundam.
- "Farneria", that, “Forneria” means oven in Italian, and it is the heart of the restaurant.
- "Lanhua" which comes from traditional Chinese dance in particular "Ballet of the East: the Flower Drum Lantern", where "A female  dancer in the ~ Flower Drum Lantern ~ is known as a Lanhua (Orchid)", and is explicitly referenced in a conversation within the game.
- "Bianca" that is a pretty common name that means "White" in Italian.
-  Etc...

Plays a lot with opposites, like:

- Real world vs Dream world.
- Kouji vs Kohji.
- Being a normal guy in the Real world. vs. Being a feared cosmic pirate in the Dream world.
- Sun vs Nuz
- Earth vs Elath.
- Space vs Contemporary Japan.
- And so..

And also the game plays 'the game' of disorienting you.

For what I have seen, the original writing plays a lot with the following concept:

“Once upon a time, I dreamt I was a butterfly, fluttering hither and thither, to all intents and purposes a butterfly. I was conscious only of my happiness as a butterfly, unaware that I was myself. Soon I awaked, and there I was, veritably myself again. Now I do not know whether I was then a man dreaming I was a butterfly, or whether I am now a butterfly, dreaming I am a man.”

~ Quote by Chuang-tzu
